## 1. What should be compared?

There are three separate questions:

1. **Candidate scoring:** if Gold is already inside Personal K5, which scorer best ranks the personal candidates?
2. **Recovery:** if Generic Top10 misses Gold but Personal K5 contains it, how often does a method successfully place Gold into the final list, and how high?
3. **End-to-end quality:** does stronger recovery improve the full IME ranking without causing excessive harmful overrides?

For the recovery stage, the most diagnostic metrics are:

- **Missing@10**: fraction of all Train-Val queries whose Gold is absent from final Top10;
- **Rec@1**: among theoretically recoverable Generic-missing cases, Gold is placed at rank 1;
- **Rec@3**: Gold is placed within Top3;
- **Rec@5**: Gold is placed within Top5;
- **Rec@10**: Gold is placed anywhere in final Top10;
- **Recovery MRR**: ranking quality over the fixed recovery population.

Overall Macro Top1 and overall MRR remain important **guardrails**, because a recovery method can improve Rec@k while damaging already-correct Generic cases.

### Fixed recovery population

$$
R=\{q:\; Gold\notin GenericTop10 \;\land\; Gold\in PersonalK5\}
$$

```text
R = { queries where Generic Top10 misses Gold AND Personal K5 contains Gold }
```

The fixed counts are:

$$
|R|=4910,\qquad GenericMissing=12565
$$

Therefore the frozen K5 theoretical recoverability ceiling is:

$$
\frac{4910}{12565}=39.08\%
$$

This ceiling is determined by the candidate surface. A scorer cannot recover a Generic-missing Gold that is not present in Personal K5.

## How recovery metrics are computed

The frozen Personal K5 surface determines whether recovery is theoretically possible.

Let:

$$
M=\{q:\ Gold\notin GenericTop10\}
$$

Plain text:

`M = queries where Generic Top10 does not contain Gold`

In Train-Val:

$$
|M|=12,565
$$

Among these Generic-missing queries, define the theoretically recoverable subset:

$$
R=\{q:\ Gold\notin GenericTop10 \land Gold\in PersonalK5\}
$$

Plain text:

`R = Generic-missing queries whose Gold is available in frozen Personal K5`

Here:

$$
|R|=4,910
$$

Therefore the frozen K5 theoretical recoverability ceiling is:

$$
\text{K5 theoretical recoverability}
=
\frac{|R|}{|M|}
=
\frac{4910}{12565}
=
39.08\%
$$

Plain text:

`39.08% of Generic-missing queries are theoretically recoverable from the frozen Personal K5 surface.`

This is a property of the candidate surface, not of a particular recovery scorer. A scorer cannot recover a Generic-missing Gold that is absent from Personal K5.

For a recovery method \(A\), realized recovery at rank \(k\) is measured only on the theoretically recoverable population \(R\):

$$
Rec@k(A)
=
\frac{
|\{q\in R:\ rank_A(Gold)\le k\}|
}{
|R|
}
$$

Plain text:

`Rec@k = among the 4,910 theoretically recoverable cases, the proportion whose Gold is placed within the final Top-k by method A.`

Therefore:

- **Rec@1**: Gold is recovered directly to rank 1.
- **Rec@3**: Gold is recovered into the final Top3.
- **Rec@5**: Gold is recovered into the final Top5.
- **Rec@10**: Gold is successfully recovered anywhere into the final Top10.

The metrics are cumulative:

$$
Rec@1 \le Rec@3 \le Rec@5 \le Rec@10
$$

For example, for **4P + 4CS + 2E**:

$$
Rec@1=\frac{1271}{4910}=25.89\%
$$

$$
Rec@3=\frac{2697}{4910}=54.93\%
$$

$$
Rec@5=\frac{3520}{4910}=71.69\%
$$

$$
Rec@10=\frac{4657}{4910}=94.85\%
$$

Thus, although only 39.08% of all Generic-missing cases are theoretically recoverable from K5, **4P + 4CS + 2E realizes 94.85% of that available recovery opportunity at Top10**.

By contrast, **Missing@10** is evaluated over the complete Train-Val population:

$$
Missing@10(A)
=
\frac{
|\{q:\ Gold\notin Top10_A(q)\}|
}{
34,416
}
$$

Plain text:

`Missing@10 = proportion of all 34,416 Train-Val queries whose final Top10 still does not contain Gold.`

The metrics therefore answer different questions:

| Metric | Population / denominator | Question |
|---|---:|---|
| **K5 theoretical recoverability** | 12,565 Generic-missing queries | Is Gold available in Personal K5 at all? |
| **Rec@1** | 4,910 theoretically recoverable queries | Did the method recover Gold to rank 1? |
| **Rec@3** | 4,910 | Did Gold reach Top3? |
| **Rec@5** | 4,910 | Did Gold reach Top5? |
| **Rec@10** | 4,910 | Did Gold enter the final Top10? |
| **Missing@10** | all 34,416 queries | How often does the complete system still fail to contain Gold in Top10? |

A useful interpretation is:

$$
\text{Candidate availability}
\rightarrow
\text{Theoretical recoverability}
\rightarrow
\text{Realized Rec@k}
\rightarrow
\text{Overall Missing@10}
$$

Plain text:

`Candidate surface determines whether recovery is possible; Rec@k measures how much of that opportunity is actually realized; Missing@10 measures the final system-wide coverage.`

## 3. PV1 and NGramSelector: what does K mean?

### 3.1 Personal K5

For each query, legal same-user same-Pinyin causal history provides up to five personal candidate targets. These form the frozen **Personal K5** pool.

For candidate `c` with historical count `n_c`, PV frequency support is:

$$
F_{PV}(c)=\frac{\log(1+n_c)}{\max_j \log(1+n_j)}
$$

```text
F_PV(c) = log(1 + n_c) / max_j log(1 + n_j)
```

### 3.2 PV1

PV1 selects **one personal candidate by frequency** and injects it using the frozen PV1 score:

$$
S_{PV1}(c)=B(q)+4F_{PV}(c)
$$

```text
S_PV1(c) = B(q) + 4 * F_PV(c)
```

where `B(q)` is the Generic/Frequency boundary score.

Pipeline:

```text
Personal history
    -> frequency ranking
    -> keep frequency Top1 personal candidate
    -> boundary + 4 * F_PV
    -> merge with Generic F
```

### 3.3 NGramSelector

NGramSelector separates **candidate identity** from **injection strength**.

```text
Frozen Personal K5
    -> Interpolated NGram ranking
    -> keep Top K personal candidates
    -> use original PV1 frequency support F_PV
    -> boundary + 4 * F_PV
    -> merge with Generic F
```

Let the NGram-selected set be:

$$
A_K(q)=TopK_{P_{NG}}(PersonalK5)
$$

For a selected personal candidate:

$$
S_{Selector-K}(c)=B(q)+4F_{PV}(c),\qquad c\in A_K(q)
$$

Interpretation:

```text
NGram decides WHO is allowed to enter.
PV frequency decides HOW STRONGLY the selected candidate enters.
```

### 3.4 Selector K1 is not PV1

- **PV1:** frequency chooses the one personal candidate.
- **Selector K1:** NGram searches across Personal K5 and chooses one contextually preferred personal candidate; frequency is then used only for injection strength.

Also, Selector `K` is **not the NGram order**. It is:

```text
K = how many personal candidates are allowed to compete
```

Thus `K` is a direct **recovery-breadth / personalisation-strength control**.

## 4. Main comparison: keep Personal K5 fixed

This is the main recovery comparison. Candidate breadth is kept at **Personal K5** so that the main difference is scoring/calibration rather than K1/K3/K5 breadth.

PV1 is included only as the historical reference; it injects one personal candidate.

### 4.1 Formula family

For the joint Context-Preference-Confidence models:

$$
S(c)=B(q)+\beta P_{NG}(c)+\gamma CS(c)+\lambda_E C_E(q)
$$

```text
S(c) = B(q) + beta * P_NG(c) + gamma * CS(c) + lambda_E * C_E(q)
```

where:

- `P_NG(c)` = Interpolated NGram contextual support;
- `CS(c)` = candidate Choice Share;
- `C_E(q) = 1 - H_norm(q)` = query-level entropy concentration/confidence.

The shorthand `4P + 4CS + 2E` therefore means:

```text
B + 4 * P_NG + 4 * CS + 2 * C_E
```

## 6. Revised conclusions

### Conclusion 1 — Best candidate scorer is not automatically the best recovery method

Q8+F is the strongest candidate-only scorer, but Interpolated NGram is much cheaper and remains strong. End-to-end recovery additionally requires calibrated injection into the Generic list.

```text
best candidate scorer != best end-to-end recovery method
```

### Conclusion 2 — Recovery should be judged mainly with recovery metrics

The strongest K5 methods differ only modestly in Overall Macro, but differ substantially in:

```text
Missing@10, Rec@1, Rec@3, Rec@5, Rec@10, Recovery MRR
```

Therefore these metrics are the main diagnostics for Stage-1 recovery. Overall Macro/MRR remain guardrails.

### Conclusion 3 — Recovery breadth and recovery ranking are different

K5+Entropy has the strongest broad recovery:

```text
Rec@3  = .6126
Rec@5  = .8035
Rec@10 = .9876
```

By contrast, `6P+2CS+.25E` has stronger recovered-candidate front ranking:

```text
Rec@1       = .2770
RecoveryMRR = .4679
```

Thus:

```text
high recoverability / availability != best recovered-candidate ordering
```

### Conclusion 4 — 4P+4CS+2E remains the balanced end-to-end development-best

`4P+4CS+2E` gives:

```text
Overall Macro = .404807
Missing@10    = .243172
Rec@1         = .2589
Rec@3         = .5493
Rec@5         = .7169
Rec@10        = .9485
Recovery MRR  = .4542
```

It does not maximize every recovery metric, but it gives the strongest balanced Overall ranking while preserving strong recovery.

### Conclusion 5 — Selector K is a clean controllability axis

Selector K directly controls how many personal candidates are allowed to compete:

```text
K1 = conservative
K3 = balanced
K5 = high coverage
```

This is scientifically useful because candidate selection and injection calibration are separated:

```text
NGram -> WHO enters
Frequency -> HOW STRONGLY it enters
```

### Conclusion 6 — The next Recovery -> Context experiment should preserve multiple Stage-1 philosophies

For the next contextual reranking stage, the most informative recovery bases are:

1. **PV1** — historical baseline;
2. **NGramSelector@K3** — balanced selective/calibrated architecture;
3. **4P+4CS+2E** — overall balanced development-best;
4. **6P+2CS+.25E** — front-rank / Top3-oriented operating point;
5. **K5+Entropy** — coverage-oriented operating point.

This allows the next experiment to answer a stronger question:

```text
When Stage 2 contextual reranking is available,
what should Stage 1 recovery optimize?

- balanced end-to-end quality?
- recovered-candidate front ranking?
- maximum candidate availability?
```

The working two-stage hypothesis is:

```text
Recovery determines availability.
Context determines ordering.
```
